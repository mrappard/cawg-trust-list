== Designation

Designation is how CAWG recognises an external organisation as a trust governance partner. CAWG decides which frameworks qualify; it does not take on the partner's operational responsibilities.

=== Types of designation

A designation has one of the following scopes:

* *Identity attestation:* the partner's credential issuers attest who a signer is, to the baseline requirements, under a stated credential-type profile.
* *Role attestation:* the partner (typically an industry body) attests what a signer is within a stated industry. A role attestation designation does not by itself make a signer's identity trusted.

A single organisation MAY hold both kinds of designation.

=== Designation criteria

To be designated for identity attestation, an applicant:

* MUST show that its framework meets every baseline requirement, under at least one credential-type profile;
* MUST publish its governing policy (for X.509, a certificate policy following RFC 3647);
* MUST have a defined process for admitting, supervising and removing credential issuers;
* MUST meet the publication requirements of the baseline requirements and the relevant profile;
* MUST have an incident-reporting process, and agree to notify CAWG of incidents affecting trust within *[TBD]*; and
* MUST have the legal and operational capacity to meet its obligations for the full designation period.

To be designated for role attestation, an applicant:

* MUST be a recognised, self-regulating body for the industry in question;
* MUST publish the criteria by which it attests the role; and
* MUST meet the publication requirements of the baseline requirements.

=== Process

. *Application:* the applicant submits its governing policy, audit evidence and a mapping of its framework to each baseline requirement.
. *Review:* a CAWG review group *[TBD: composition and conflict-of-interest rules]* assesses the application and may ask for more information.
. *Decision:* CAWG grants or refuses designation, with reasons.
. *Agreement:* CAWG and the partner sign a designation agreement setting out the scope, period and terms.
. *Publication:* CAWG adds the designation to the designation register, with its start date.

=== Designation terms

A designation agreement MUST state:

* the scope (identity or role attestation, credential-type profiles, and any industry or geography);
* the designation period, which MUST NOT exceed *[TBD]* before renewal;
* the partner's obligations under the baseline requirements;
* incident-reporting and cooperation obligations; and
* the grounds and process for suspension and withdrawal.

=== Suspension and withdrawal

CAWG MAY suspend or withdraw a designation if the partner stops meeting the designation criteria, fails to report or remedy an incident, or asks to withdraw. CAWG MAY reinstate a suspended designation once the cause is remedied.

Suspension, reinstatement and withdrawal:

* MUST be recorded in the designation register as a change to the designation's intervals (see <<_designation_register>>), with the date and time from which the change takes effect;
* MUST take effect from the recorded time onwards, and MUST NOT be backdated. Identity assertions whose trusted time of signing falls inside an interval in which the designation was in effect remain trusted (subject to the credential's own status); and
* for withdrawal, SHOULD be matched by the partner giving its trust anchor configurations a corresponding `notAfter`. Validators do not depend on this, because they enforce the register's intervals directly (<<_validator_requirements>>).

NOTE: Suspension and withdrawal are not the mechanism for invalidating past signatures. For an individual credential, the credential issuer records an invalidity date (<<_status_and_revocation>>). Where the partner or an issuer cannot be relied on to do that, CAWG uses emergency distrust.

=== Emergency distrust

Emergency distrust lets CAWG stop validators trusting a specific trust anchor or issuing CA from a stated time, which may be in the past, without the cooperation of the affected partner. It is for situations in which a partner or credential issuer cannot be relied on to publish revocations, for example:

* a trust anchor or issuing CA private key is compromised;
* a credential issuer has mis-issued credentials and has not revoked them; or
* a partner or issuer is acting in bad faith or has stopped operating.

An emergency distrust entry:

* MUST identify the affected trust anchor or CA certificate by the SHA-256 hash of the certificate;
* MUST carry a *distrust-after* date/time. Identity assertions whose trusted time of signing is at or after that time MUST NOT be trusted through the affected certificate. The distrust-after time MAY be earlier than the time the entry is published, and SHOULD be set to the earliest time from which trust can no longer be relied on;
* MUST record the reason and the date the entry was published; and
* MUST NOT be removed once published. If it was made in error, CAWG publishes a superseding entry with a later distrust-after time and the reason for the change.

Emergency distrust is published in the designation register, which MUST be signed when it contains distrust entries, so that validators can authenticate them. It applies whatever the affected partner's own trust list says.

CAWG MUST define, before the first designation, who may invoke emergency distrust, what evidence is required, how quickly an entry can be published, and how the affected partner can respond or appeal. Because a backdated distrust-after time can invalidate legitimate signatures, the evidence and reasoning for each entry SHOULD be published, and an entry SHOULD be reviewed after it is published.

Emergency distrust applies to a specific certificate. CAWG MAY also suspend or withdraw the partner's designation, which takes effect from that time onwards.

=== Designation register

CAWG publishes a designation register listing, for each current and past designation:

* the partner's name and scope;
* one or more *designation intervals*: each with a start date/time and, if it has ended, an end date/time. A suspension ends an interval; a reinstatement starts a new one. Withdrawal ends the last interval;
* the location of the partner's published trust list;
* the applicable credential-type profile; and
* any emergency distrust entries for the partner's trust anchors or issuing CAs (<<_emergency_distrust>>).

The designation register is authoritative for when a designation is in effect. A partner's own trust anchor dates can narrow that period but cannot extend it.

The register MUST be published at a stable HTTPS URL, SHOULD be signed (and MUST be signed when it contains emergency distrust entries), and MUST keep past intervals and distrust entries rather than deleting them, so that historical signatures can be evaluated.

The register is a record of CAWG's recognition decisions. It is not itself a trust list, and CAWG does not operate the partners' lists.
