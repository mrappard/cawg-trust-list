== Governance

=== List operator

The list operator maintains, signs and publishes the CAWG Trust List. Candidates are DIF, a CAWG committee, or an operator acting under delegation from either. The list operator:

* MUST maintain a public change log of additions, `notAfter` changes and other modifications;
* MUST provide participating CAs with a means to confirm CAWG membership and receive notice when membership ends; and
* SHOULD give advance notice of changes that remove trust.

=== Issuance model

The working group needs to choose between two issuance models:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Model | Advantages | Disadvantages

| Single CAWG-operated CA
| Simplest to launch; membership vetting and revocation are under direct CAWG control.
| CAWG or DIF becomes a CA operator, with audit, key ceremony and 24/7 revocation obligations.

| Multiple participating commercial CAs (C2PA model)
| Scales; members can use CAs they already work with; operational burden sits with CAs.
| Needs CA admission criteria, audit review and ongoing oversight by the list operator.
|===

=== CA admission

To be added to the CAWG Trust List, a CA:

* MUST publish a certification practice statement that conforms to the CAWG certificate policy;
* MUST hold a current audit report from a recognised audit scheme (for example, WebTrust for CAs or ETSI EN 319 411-1), whose scope covers the trust anchor and issuing CAs;
* MUST operate an OCSP responder for subscriber certificates; and
* MUST agree to the incident reporting and revocation timelines set by the list operator.

=== CA removal

The list operator MAY end trust in a participating CA for failure to meet the admission requirements, a security incident, or at the CA's request. Trust MUST be ended by setting `notAfter` on the CA's trust anchor configuration, not by deleting it.
