== Baseline requirements

These requirements apply to every trust governance partner designated for identity attestation, whatever the credential type. Credential-type profiles (<<_credential_type_profiles>>) say how each requirement is met for a particular credential type.

=== Identity verification

* A credential issuer MUST verify the legal existence and name of an organisational subject using reliable, independent sources, to a level at least equivalent to organisation validation under the CA/Browser Forum S/MIME Baseline Requirements.
* A credential issuer that names an individual MUST verify that individual's identity to a level at least equivalent to individual validation under the same requirements.
* If an individual acts on behalf of an organisation, the credential issuer MUST verify the organisation's authorisation (comparable to sponsor validation).
* A credential issuer SHOULD record a globally unique organisational identifier, such as a Legal Entity Identifier (LEI), where one exists.
* A credential issuer MUST NOT issue a credential based on mailbox or domain control alone.

=== Credential content

* A credential MUST identify the subject by its verified legal name.
* A credential MUST NOT contain role attestations (such as "news organisation") unless the issuer is also designated for role attestation in that scope.
* A credential MUST be usable only for signing; credentials that can also issue other credentials MUST NOT be used to sign identity assertions.

=== Key protection

* Signer private keys SHOULD be generated and held in hardware-backed key storage.
* A credential issuer MUST revoke a credential on evidence of key compromise.

=== Status and revocation

* Every credential MUST identify a means by which validators can discover its current status. A credential with no discoverable status method does not meet these requirements.
* A credential issuer MUST keep status information available for every credential for at least *[TBD]* after the credential expires, so that revocations made after expiry can still be discovered.
* A credential issuer MUST revoke a credential within *[TBD]* of learning that it was issued in error, its key was compromised, or the subject's verified information is no longer accurate.
* A credential issuer MUST record the reason for every revocation.

The effect of revocation depends on the reason:

* *Routine revocation* (the subject's details changed, its relationship with the issuer ended, or the credential was replaced) MUST NOT invalidate identity assertions that a trusted time stamp shows were signed before the revocation time.
* *Revocation for key compromise or mis-issuance* MUST carry an *invalidity date*. Identity assertions whose trusted time of signing is at or after the invalidity date MUST be treated as untrusted, even if they were time-stamped before the revocation time.

The invalidity date MUST be set as follows:

* *Mis-issuance:* the start of the credential's validity period (for X.509, `notBefore`), so that no signature made with the credential is trusted.
* *Key compromise:* the earliest time the key is known or suspected to have been compromised, or the start of the credential's validity period, whichever is later. If the key was compromised before the credential became valid, the invalidity date is therefore the start of the validity period.

NOTE: Signatures made before the start of a credential's validity period are already outside its validity, so setting the invalidity date no earlier than that start loses nothing.

A credential issuer MUST keep the evidence on which an invalidity date is based, and MUST provide it to its trust governance partner on request, so that the date can be reviewed and an issuer cannot invalidate legitimate signatures without cause.

If a trust governance partner or a credential issuer cannot be relied on to publish revocations (for example, because it is itself compromised), CAWG can act independently through emergency distrust (<<_emergency_distrust>>).

=== Audit and supervision

* A trust governance partner MUST supervise the credential issuers in its scope and remove those that stop meeting these requirements.
* Credential issuers MUST be audited at least annually against a recognised audit scheme appropriate to the credential type (for example, WebTrust for CAs or ETSI EN 319 411-1 for X.509), or an equivalent the designation agreement accepts.

=== Publication

* A trust governance partner MUST publish its trust list at a stable HTTPS URL, in the form required by the relevant credential-type profile.
* A trust governance partner MUST keep a public change log, and MUST end trust in an issuer by recording an end date rather than deleting its entry, so that past signatures remain verifiable.
* A trust governance partner SHOULD sign its published trust list.
